## 🧬 Human-Making Magic

First of all, is this a time-consuming process?

Yes.

Is it worth every single second spent and then some?

Big yes.

You can spend as much time on this as you want, but I can’t over-emphasise the value this writing will add to your writing in the long term. Trust me, you will find yourself coming back to your character sheet again and again and again in the process of writing your drafts, and it helps loads.

Stupid small details like your character’s favourite food *matter*. You might reference them twice over the course of your ten-book series, but when you mention them, they need to be consistent. Even though specific details might seem trivial, none of them is (the difference between a character whose favourite food is McDonald’s and one whose is anchovy and tomato tostada is significant).

This is my process. Feel free to take what you like and leave what you don’t. This resource, your character sheet, is unique to *you* and your work, so make it work any way you want.

### 🎨 Finding your Character Image

I like to start with a photo of the character. A lot of writers I know have an idea of who they would cast in their dream casting for their characters. We stumbled upon Sophie Turner from Game of Thrones for our lead character of Lucinda Sharp, and her photo has been on the *Seen in Silverbridge Hero Sheet* since we started.

Look through Pinterest. Google search. Hell, use Bing if you want. If your character doesn’t look like any actors that you know off the top of your head, I find that scrolling through Instagram and Facebook helps. There are a lot of people out there, so if you look for long enough, you’ll find the perfect person.

### 🎻 Who Is This Person?

There are a bunch of variables that make up a person’s character, and you can split them up into two categories: **Material** and **Intangible**.

Favourite sport, favA Rockstar Has Explodednguishing physical traits…. These can all be measured. But how does someone react when they’re cornered? How does somebody deal with bad news? What’s a formative memory from their childhood? Those are just as important, maybe more so, and a lot harder to pin down.

This is where you stretch your creative muscle. And don’t just write down the first thing you think of. Really consider every one of these variables because they all contribute to making your character feel more like your Character**,** with a capital C and in italics. They become themselves.

A lot of the time, the character will dictate their likes and dislikes and formative experiences for you. It just … makes sense. That’s a treat when that happens. But most of the time, it’s up to you to comb through your breadth of experiences and knowledge about how people work and choose what feels like for your character.

Here’s the list of details I use:

Nickname, Age, Birthday, Ethnicity/Country of Birth, Height, Residence, Relationship Status, Siblings, Parents, Children, Job, Email, Socials, Distinguishing physical traits, Personality traits, Education, Likes, Dislikes, Favourite sport/exercise, Ambitions, Favourite music, Religion/Spiritual beliefs, What they do in their spare time? What would their dream job be? Who is their favourite celebrity? Favourite food, Favourite drink, Favourite clothes, What do they fear? What do they chase?

That looks like a daunting list, I know! But once you start, it is fun. If you struggle to fill out every last thing, I suggest using AI to help. I often write a few prompts into ChatGPT to get ideas flowing. For example:

## 🧪 Cook Up A Character

If I go through all of these steps and fill out all of those details for each main character in a book I’m writing, I fly through the draft. I can open my spreadsheet whenever I need a refresher, and there is everything I could ever need. Getting to know the amorphous characters inside your head becomes easy once you’ve done this exercise.

Go forth and make your own character sheet!
